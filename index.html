Comparative Product Teardown: ChatGPT vs Claude vs Gemini

A comparative product teardown across monetization, feature prioritization, UX simplicity and API strategy.

The AI race is usually framed as a model race. Which model reasons are better? Which one codes better? Which one performs better on benchmarks? But as the underlying models become more capable, model performance is becoming only one part of the product. The more interesting question is what happens after the model becomes good enough.

I looked at ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini through four product lenses: monetisation, feature prioritisation, UX simplicity, and API strategy. What emerged is less a story about three similar AI assistants and more a story about three different approaches to building an AI business.

Three Products, Three Different Bets:

  • ChatGPT is building breadth. OpenAI is expanding ChatGPT from a consumer assistant into a broader AI platform spanning subscriptions, coding, agents, enterprise workflows, advertising, and APIs.
  • Claude is building depth. Anthropic is concentrating more heavily on developers, coding, and professional workflows, where intensive usage and enterprise adoption can translate directly into revenue.
  • Gemini is building distribution. Google has something the others cannot easily replicate: an existing ecosystem across Search, Android, Chrome, Workspace, and Cloud. That gives Gemini a different path to value. It does not have to succeed only as a standalone AI product. It can become part of products people already use every day.

Monetisation Is Becoming a Product Decision

AI has a difficult monetisation problem. Greater usage increases product value, but intensive usage of frontier models also increases infrastructure costs. Pricing is therefore not simply a financial decision. It is a product decision.

ChatGPT has built a broad monetisation structure across free users, consumer subscriptions, business, enterprise, and API customers. Claude is more focused on capturing value from increasingly intensive users through its progression from free access to Pro, Max, Team, Enterprise, and API usage. Gemini has a different advantage: Google can use AI to increase the value of an ecosystem that already has enormous reach rather than relying entirely on standalone AI subscriptions.

The Feature Race Is Really a Prioritisation Problem

The original AI assistant had a simple mental model: ask a question and get an answer. That model is changing. AI products are increasingly combining research, reasoning, tools, creation, coding, iteration, and execution into a single workflow. More capability creates more opportunities to deliver value, but it also creates more complexity.

ChatGPT is expanding horizontally, adding more tools, workflows, agents, and use cases within one platform. Claude appears more concentrated around professional and developer workflows, particularly coding and agentic tasks. Gemini has another lever: integration. Its capabilities become more useful when they connect naturally with the Google products and services users already work with.

The UX Problem: Capability vs Simplicity

AI products are becoming significantly more capable, but that capability comes with a UX cost. Users increasingly need to understand which model or tool to use, when to use research, what context is available, what an agent is doing, and where the final output will appear. A 2026 MeasuringU study of 420 U.S. participants found that ChatGPT recorded the highest measured UX-Lite and SUS scores among the products tested, although the differences were not statistically significant.

The more interesting insight is not the ranking. It is the underlying problem: AI products are accumulating capabilities faster than users can build mental models around them. The next UX advantage may come from reducing the complexity users have to manage without taking away their control.

APIs Are Becoming Distribution Channels

The consumer interface is only one layer of an AI product. Developers are building applications, workflows, and businesses on top of these models, which makes API strategy strategically important. OpenAI is building a broad developer platform around models, tools, agents, and APIs. Anthropic is connecting Claude closely to developer workflows, with products such as Claude Code turning model capability into a practical professional workflow. Google can connect Gemini to Cloud and its existing enterprise ecosystem. The API is therefore more than another revenue channel; it is also a distribution channel.

The Competition Is Moving Beyond the Model

Looking across these areas, the strategies become clearer. ChatGPT is expanding the scope of the AI experience. Claude is going deeper into high-value professional workflows. Gemini is using distribution and ecosystem integration to put AI into more of the products people already use. These strategies do not depend entirely on permanent differences in model capability. If model performance continues to converge, differentiation will increasingly come from distribution, workflow integration, developer ecosystems, switching costs, user habits, and monetisation. The product can become more important than the model.

The Metric That Matters More Than Response Time

Most AI products make it easy to focus on time to response. I think a more useful metric is time to usable outcome. The user does not necessarily want an answer. They want what that answer helps them accomplish: a piece of research, a report, a presentation, a decision, a piece of code, or a completed workflow.

That changes how the product should be measured. Instead of looking only at response quality and response time, we can measure the journey from user intent to completed outcome by tracking interventions, prompt reformulations, manual edits, time to final output, and where the workflow breaks.

From AI Assistant to Work System

The AI assistant is evolving from a place where users ask questions into a system that helps them complete work. That creates a new UX challenge: as AI becomes more autonomous, users may need to interact with it less while simultaneously needing greater visibility into what it is doing (e.g., Research completed, Analysis in progress, Synthesis underway, Final output pending). The goal is not to expose every system process, but to give users enough visibility to understand where the work stands and enough control to intervene when necessary.

CTRL + THINK

ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini are increasingly operating at a similar technological frontier, but they are taking different approaches to turning that technology into a product. ChatGPT is building breadth. Claude is building depth. Gemini is building distribution.

The next phase of AI competition may therefore be determined less by who has the strongest model at a given moment and more by who can turn increasingly capable AI into a valuable, repeatable, and scalable workflow. The AI product is moving beyond helping users think. It is increasingly becoming responsible for what happens after thinking.
